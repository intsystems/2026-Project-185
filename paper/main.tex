\begin{abstract}

Operator learning methods typically approximate the solution map of a physical system with a single global model — an assumption that fails when solutions change character fundamentally across the parameter space.

We propose TEMPO: a framework that identifies latent dynamical regimes in snapshot data via Expectation-Maximization, builds a dedicated Neural-POD basis for each, and learns to route new inputs to the right regime at inference time. On multi-regime benchmarks, TEMPO achieves substantially lower errors than POD-DeepONet and vanilla DeepONet, with interpretable structure that reflects the underlying physics.

\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} Operator learning, DeepONet, Proper Orthogonal Decomposition, Expectation-Maximization, Gaussian mixture models, Regime discovery

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Operator learning addresses the approximation of mappings between infinite-dimensional function spaces, arising naturally in the solution of parametric partial differential equations (PDEs) governing physical systems~\cite{Kovachki2024}. This paradigm has demonstrated substantial promise across both  theoretical analysis~\cite{Kovachki2024, Lu2019} and practical applications including weather modeling, quantum systems, fluid and sound dynamics~\cite{Pathak2022, Olivieri2024,  Dave2025, Tucker2025}.

Among the most prominent architectures, Deep Operator Networks (DeepONet)~\cite{Lu2019} approximate operators via paired branch and trunk networks, where the branch encodes the input function and the trunk encodes the query location.The Fourier Neural Operator (FNO)~\cite{Li2020} independently parameterizes the solution operator in the spectral domain, offering resolution-invariance and strong empirical performance. POD-DeepONet~\cite{Lu2022} improves upon standard DeepONet by replacing the learned trunk network with a fixed basis obtained from Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) of training snapshots, yielding more compact and interpretable representations

However, a single global POD basis is inherently limited for problems whose solutions exhibit qualitatively distinct dynamical regimes — such as solutions transitioning between laminar and turbulent behavior, or dispersive waves with parameter-dependent structure. In such settings, the optimal basis varies across the solution manifold, and a single linear subspace cannot represent the full solution diversity.

Several approaches enrich the POD basis to overcome this limitation. Sharma and Shankar~\cite{Sharma2025} update the DeepONet trunk with multiple complementary bases — precomputed POD modes and a partition-of-unity mixture-of-experts (PoU-MoE) trunk, trained jointly with a single branch. On 2D and 3D problems with sharp spatial gradients, the POD-PoU ensemble achieves up to 2$\times$ lower errors over POD-DeepONet~\cite{Lu2022} and 2--4$\times$ over vanilla DeepONet.


Moreover, the linear structure of the POD basis is insufficient for strongly nonlinear solution manifolds. Mou et al.~\cite{Mou2026} propose Neural-POD, which constructs nonlinear orthogonal basis functions via iterative residual minimization analogous to Gram--Schmidt orthogonalization, representing each  spatial mode and temporal component as neural networks trained sequentially on the residual variance.


Yet both approaches enrich the basis without identifying the underlying dynamical regimes present in the data. This idea has been pursued in the reduced order modeling (ROM) literature, where snapshot ensembles are partitioned into clusters and a separate local POD basis is constructed for each~\cite{Amsallem2012, Hess2019}. More recently, Daniel et al.~\cite{Daniel2020} extended this paradigm by clustering solution subspaces on the Grassmann manifold and training a deep neural network to select the appropriate local model from a dictionary at inference time. However, these methods operate offline on fixed snapshot sets, employ hard cluster assignments, and do not integrate regime discovery with operator learning in an end-to-end trainable architecture.

In this work, we propose \textbf{TEMPO} (\textit{Regime-Aware Operator Learning with Locally Adapted POD Bases}). Our method integrates regime discovery directly into operator learning: a Gaussian mixture model over Neural-POD coefficient space identifies latent dynamical regimes via soft EM, constructing a dedicated locally adapted basis for each. At inference time, a learned gating network selects among regimes, producing predictions that are accurate across the full parameter space.


\section{Problem Statement}

The behavior of many physical systems is governed by a set of parameters $\kappa \in \cK \subset \R^{d_\kappa}$: viscosity controls whether flow is laminar or turbulent, boundary forcing shapes wave patterns, material coefficients determine whether stress propagates smoothly or develops shocks. As $\kappa$ varies across the parameter space $\cK$, the corresponding solutions can change not just quantitatively but qualitatively, demanding fundamentally different representations in each regime. Predicting these solutions accurately and instantly, without re-running the underlying simulator, is the central problem we address.

Let $\Omega \subset \R^d$ be a bounded spatial domain and $\T \subset \R_{\geq 0}$ a time interval.

We are given a dataset of $N$ solution snapshots:
\begin{equation}
  \cD = \bigl\{\,(u^{(i)}_0,\,\kappa^{(i)},\,t^{(i)},\,s^{(i)})\,\bigr\}_{i=1}^{N},
  \qquad
  s^{(i)}(\cdot) = s\!\left(\cdot,\,t^{(i)};\,u^{(i)}_0,\kappa^{(i)}\right)
  \in L^2(\Omega),
  \label{eq:dataset}
\end{equation}
where $u^{(i)} \in \mathcal{U} = L^2(\Omega)$ is the input function, $\kappa^{(i)} \in \cK$ the physical parameter, $t^{(i)} \in \T$ the observation time, and $s^{(i)}$ the corresponding solution discretized on $N_y$ quadrature points $\{(x_j, w_j)\}_{j=1}^{N_y}$ with norm $\normsq{f} = \sum_j w_j f(x_j)^2$.


The dataset $\cD$ captures $\cG$ through a finite collection of snapshots; the underlying operator is defined as follows.

\begin{definition}[Solution operator]
\label{def:operator}
The \emph{solution operator}
 \[ \cG: \mathcal{U} \times \cK \to L^2(\Omega \times \T) \]
maps each pair $(u_0,\kappa)$ to the full space-time solution:
$\cG(u_0,\kappa) = s(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,;\,u_0,\kappa)$.
\end{definition}


The operator $\cG$ is unknown and expensive to evaluate directly. The \emph{operator learning problem} is to construct $\hat{\cG} \approx \cG$ from $\cD$ alone, generalizing to unseen $(u,\kappa) \notin \cD$ without solving the PDE.





\subsection*{The multi-regime assumption}

When solutions change character fundamentally across $\cK$, the optimal low-dimensional representation differs from regime to regime, and no single global basis captures the full solution diversity. We formalize this observation via a latent variable model over the snapshot ensemble.

\begin{definition}[Generative model]
\label{def:generative}
Each snapshot arises from one of $M$ latent regimes. The regime label $z_i \in \{1,\ldots,M\}$ is unobserved and drawn from a categorical prior,
\begin{equation}
  z_i \sim \mathrm{Categorical}(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_M),
  \qquad \pi_m \geq 0,\quad \textstyle\sum_m \pi_m = 1.
  \label{eq:prior}
\end{equation}
Conditioned on $z_i = m$, the snapshot is distributed as
\begin{equation}
 ( s^{(i)} \mid z_i{=}m) \;\sim\;
  \cN\!\bigl(\hats_m^{(i)},\;\sigma^2 W^{-1}\bigr),
  \label{eq:likelihood}
\end{equation}
where $W = \mathrm{diag}(w_1,\ldots,w_{N_y})$ is the quadrature weight matrix, $\sigma^2 > 0$ controls the softness of regime assignments, and $\hats_m^{(i)}$ is the regime-$m$ approximation to $s^{(i)}$.
\end{definition}


The generative model leaves $\hats_m^{(i)}$ unspecified. We require only that it admits a \emph{separable low-rank form}:
\begin{equation}
  \hats_m^{(i)}(x)
  \;=\; \phi_0^{(m)}\!\left(x;\kappa^{(i)}\right)
  + \sum_{p=1}^{P_m}
    \phi_p^{(m)}\!\left(x;\kappa^{(i)}\right)\,
    \psi_p^{(m)}\!\left(t^{(i)};\kappa^{(i)}\right),
  \label{eq:separable}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_p^{(m)}(\cdot;\kappa)$ are spatial basis functions and $\psi_p^{(m)}(\cdot;\kappa)$ temporal coefficients, both conditioned on $\kappa$. This form encompasses POD~\citep{Lu2022}, kernel PCA~\citep{Eivazi2024}, and learned neural bases~\citep{Mou2026}; in this work we adopt the latter.

\begin{definition}[Regime approximator]
\label{def:approx}
The parameters of regime $m$ are
$\Phi_m = \{\phi_p^{(m)}, \psi_p^{(m)}\}_{p=0}^{P_m}$,
where $\phi_0^{(m)}(\cdot;\kappa)$ is a parameter-dependent mean,
$\{\phi_p^{(m)}\}_{p \geq 1}$ spatial basis networks,
$\{\psi_p^{(m)}\}_{p \geq 1}$ temporal coefficient networks,
and $P_m \leq P_{\max}$ the adaptive mode count.
\end{definition}

The learning problem decomposes into two phases: discovering the latent regime structure from $\cD$ \emph{offline}, then training a deployable operator for fast inference \emph{online}.


\subsection*{Phase 1: Offline regime discovery}

Since the regime labels $z_i$ in~\eqref{eq:likelihood} are unobserved,
we recover the bases $\{\Phi_m\}$, mixture weights $\pi$, and noise
level $\sigma^2$ by maximizing the observed-data log-likelihood:
\begin{equation}
  (\Phi^*,\pi^*,\sigma^{2*})
  \;=\; \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\Phi,\,\pi,\,\sigma^2}
  \;\sum_{i=1}^N \log \sum_{m=1}^M
    \frac{\pi_m\,\det(W)^{1/2}}{(2\pi\sigma^2)^{N_y/2}}
    \exp\!\!\left(-\frac{\normsq{s^{(i)}-\hats_m^{(i)}}}{2\sigma^2}\right).
  \label{eq:mle}
\end{equation}
The solution yields soft regime assignments
$\gamma_{im}^* = p(z_i{=}m \mid s^{(i)},\Phi^*,\pi^*,\sigma^{2*})$
that encode the discovered regime structure of $\cD$
and serve as supervision for Phase~2.



\subsection*{Phase 2: Online operator learning}

With bases $\{\Phi_m^*\}$ and responsibilities $\{\gamma_{im}^*\}$
frozen, we train a \emph{GatingNet} and $M$ \emph{BranchNets} that
map a new input $(u, \kappa)$ to a weighted combination of local
regime predictions. The training objective balances three goals:
reconstruction accuracy on $\cD$, consistency of the learned gating
with the offline regime structure $\{\gamma_{im}^*\}$, and diversity
across regimes to prevent collapse. The precise architecture and loss
are given in Section~\ref{sec:theory}.

The quality of the final approximation is measured by the mean
relative $L^2$ error on held-out test snapshots:
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon \;=\; \frac{1}{N_\mathrm{test}}
  \sum_{i=1}^{N_\mathrm{test}}
  \frac{\norm{s^{(i)} - \hat{s}(\cdot;\,u^{(i)},\kappa^{(i)})}}
       {\norm{s^{(i)}}} .
  \label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}

\textit{The algorithm} solves Phase~1 via EM and 
Phase~2 via gradient descent; details are in Section~\ref{sec:theory}.

\section{Theory}\label{sec:theory}



All methods studied in this work share a common two-phase structure:
an \emph{offline} basis-fitting phase that constructs a low-rank
approximation to the solution manifold from snapshots in $\mathcal{D}$,
and an \emph{online} operator-learning phase that trains a branch
network to predict expansion coefficients from a new input
$(u_0, \kappa)$.
The four methods differ along two independent axes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Basis type.}
    A \emph{linear} (POD) basis uses a global SVD of the snapshot
    matrix; a \emph{nonlinear} (NeuralPOD) basis learns spatiotemporal
    mode networks by sequential residual minimization.
  \item \textbf{Number of regimes.}
    A \emph{single-regime} operator fits one global basis to all
    snapshots; a \emph{multi-regime} operator (TEMPO) discovers $M$
    latent regimes via EM and assigns a dedicated basis to each.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
This gives four combinations:
POD-DeepONet~\cite{Lu2022} (linear, global),
NeuralPOD-DeepONet (nonlinear, global, our extension of~\cite{Mou2026}),
TEMPO(POD) (linear, $M$ regimes), and
TEMPO(NeuralPOD) (nonlinear, $M$ regimes).
Sections~\ref{sec:bases}--\ref{sec:online} develop each component.


\subsection{Basis Representations}\label{sec:bases}


\subsubsection{POD Basis}

Given $N$ training snapshots arranged as columns of
$S \in \mathbb{R}^{N_y \times N}$, the rank-$K$ truncated SVD
$S \approx U_K \Sigma_K V_K^\top$
yields orthonormal spatial modes $\{\phi_k\}_{k=1}^K$
(columns of $U_K$) and scalar coefficients
$\lambda_k^{(i)} = [\Sigma_K V_K^\top]_{ki}$.
The approximation
\begin{equation}
  \hat{s}^{(i)}(x,t)
  = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \lambda_k^{(i)}\,\phi_k(x,t)
  \label{eq:pod_approx}
\end{equation}
is optimal in the $L^2$ sense among all rank-$K$ linear representations.
The basis does not depend on $\kappa$: a single set of directions must
represent solutions across the entire parameter space $\mathcal{K}$.

\subsubsection{NeuralPOD Basis}\label{sec:neuralpod_basis}

To overcome the linearity constraint, we parametrize each basis
function as an MLP $\phi_k : \Omega \times \mathcal{T} \to \mathbb{R}$
and learn scalar coefficients $\{\lambda_k^{(i)}\}$ jointly by
\emph{greedy residual minimization}~\cite{Mou2026}.
Let $r^{(0),i} = s^{(i)} - \phi_0$, where $\phi_0$ is a learned mean
network.
For each subsequent mode $k \ge 1$, given the residual
\begin{equation}
  r^{(k-1),i}(x,t)
  = s^{(i)}(x,t)
  - \phi_0(x,t)
  - \sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\phi_j(x,t)\,\lambda_j^{(i)},
  \label{eq:residual_global}
\end{equation}
we solve
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\phi_k,\,\{\lambda_k^{(i)}\}}
  \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
  \bigl\|r^{(k-1),i} - \phi_k\cdot\lambda_k^{(i)}\bigr\|^2.
  \label{eq:neuralpod_loss}
\end{equation}
Mode addition continues until the mean residual norm falls below
$\mathrm{Tol}$ or $k = K_{\max}$.
This procedure is the nonlinear analogue of Gram--Schmidt
orthogonalization in function space: each mode extracts the largest
component of the variance unexplained by the previous modes, without
constraining those components to lie in a fixed linear subspace.

\subsection{Single-Regime Operators}\label{sec:single}


\subsubsection{POD-DeepONet}

POD-DeepONet~\cite{Lu2022} replaces the learned trunk network of
DeepONet~\cite{Lu2019} with the fixed POD basis.
After computing the global modes $\{\phi_k\}_{k=1}^K$ and training
coefficients $\{\lambda^{(i)}\}$ offline, a branch network
$\mathcal{B}_\theta : \mathbb{R}^{m_s + d_\kappa} \to \mathbb{R}^K$
is trained online by regression against these coefficients:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{branch}}
  = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
    \bigl\|\mathcal{B}_\theta(u_0^{(i)},\,\kappa^{(i)})
    - \lambda^{(i)}\bigr\|^2.
  \label{eq:pod_branch_loss}
\end{equation}
The prediction at any $(x,t) \in \Omega \times \mathcal{T}$ is
\begin{equation}
  \hat{s}(x,t;\,u_0,\kappa)
  = \phi_0(x,t)
  + \sum_{k=1}^{K}
    \mathcal{B}_{\theta,k}(u_0,\kappa)\,\phi_k(x,t).
  \label{eq:pod_predict}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{NeuralPOD-DeepONet}\label{sec:neuralpod_deeponet}

Mou et al.~\cite{Mou2026} introduce NeuralPOD as a dimensionality
reduction for PDE solutions, establishing the greedy basis-fitting
procedure of Section~\ref{sec:neuralpod_basis}.
However, they do not construct a deployable solution operator: no
network is proposed to predict the mode coefficients for an unseen
initial condition.
We close this gap by substituting the NeuralPOD basis into the
POD-DeepONet framework.

\paragraph{Phase 1 (offline).}
Train the global NeuralPOD basis
$\{\phi_0, \phi_1, \ldots, \phi_K\}$
on all $N$ training snapshots by the procedure of
Section~\ref{sec:neuralpod_basis}, obtaining coefficients
$\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^K$ for each snapshot.

\paragraph{Phase 2 (online).}
Fix the NeuralPOD basis. Train a branch network
$\mathcal{B}_\theta : \mathbb{R}^{m_s + d_\kappa} \to \mathbb{R}^K$
by minimizing~\eqref{eq:pod_branch_loss} with the NeuralPOD
coefficients as regression targets.

\paragraph{Prediction.}
The inference formula is identical to~\eqref{eq:pod_predict}, with
the NeuralPOD networks substituted for the POD modes.
Because the nonlinear basis captures solution structure that lies
off any fixed linear subspace, this operator spans a strictly richer
function space than POD-DeepONet at the same mode count $K$.


\subsection{TEMPO: Offline Regime Discovery}\label{sec:offline}


A single global basis — whether linear or nonlinear — must compromise
between qualitatively distinct solution regimes.
For problems such as Burgers' equation, where small viscosity produces
sharp shock structures and large viscosity yields smooth diffusion, the
optimal basis geometry differs fundamentally between regimes.
TEMPO avoids this compromise by constructing a dedicated basis for each
of $M$ latent regimes, discovered from unlabelled snapshots via
Expectation--Maximization on the generative model of
Definition~\ref{def:generative}.

Under regime $m$, the NeuralPOD approximation takes a
\emph{parameter-conditioned separable form}:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{s}_m^{(i)}(x)
  = \phi_0^{(m)}\!\left(x;\kappa^{(i)}\right)
  + \sum_{p=1}^{P_m}
    \phi_p^{(m)}\!\left(x;\kappa^{(i)}\right)
    \psi_p^{(m)}\!\left(t^{(i)};\kappa^{(i)}\right),
  \label{eq:neuralpod_regime}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_p^{(m)}(\cdot;\kappa)$ and $\psi_p^{(m)}(\cdot;\kappa)$ are
separate spatial and temporal networks conditioned on $\kappa$.
This differs from the single-regime basis of Section~\ref{sec:neuralpod_basis}
in two respects: the basis is \emph{split across space and time}
(enabling the branch network to supply only scalar coefficients per mode),
and it is \emph{conditioned on $\kappa$}, so that the same networks
generalize continuously across the full parameter space $\mathcal{K}$
without retraining.

\paragraph{Initialization.}
A global POD is computed on all $N$ snapshots; each snapshot is
projected to a $P$-dimensional coefficient vector
$\alpha^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^P$.
Running a standard GMM on $\{\alpha^{(i)}\}$ yields initial
responsibilities $\gamma_{im}^{(0)}$, weights $\pi_m^{(0)}$,
and per-regime statistics $(\mu_m^{(0)}, \Sigma_m^{(0)})$.
Each regime basis $\Phi_m^{(0)}$ is initialized by
\textsc{WeightedNeuralPOD} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:npod}) with weights
$\{\gamma_{im}^{(0)}\}$.

\paragraph{E-step.}
At iteration $t$, the posterior regime responsibility for snapshot $i$
follows directly from Bayes' theorem applied to the Gaussian likelihood
of Definition~\ref{def:generative}:
\begin{equation}
  \gamma_{im}^{(t)}
  = \frac{
    \pi_m^{(t-1)}\,
    \exp\!\Bigl(-\dfrac{\|s^{(i)}-\hat{s}_m^{(i)}\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\Bigr)
  }{
    \sum_{j=1}^{M}
    \pi_j^{(t-1)}\,
    \exp\!\Bigl(-\dfrac{\|s^{(i)}-\hat{s}_j^{(i)}\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\Bigr)
  },
  \label{eq:estep}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{s}_m^{(i)}$ is evaluated via~\eqref{eq:neuralpod_regime}
using $\Phi_m^{(t-1)}$ and $\kappa^{(i)}$.
Crucially, as the NeuralPOD bases improve in the M-step, the
reconstructions $\hat{s}_m^{(i)}$ sharpen, and the E-step
automatically refines the regime assignments in response.
This coupling distinguishes TEMPO from a GMM applied to a fixed
feature space.

\paragraph{M-step.}
Maximizing the expected complete-data log-likelihood with
responsibilities fixed yields a closed-form update for the mixture
weights,
\begin{equation}
  \pi_m^{(t)} \leftarrow \frac{N_m^{(t)}}{N},
  \qquad N_m^{(t)} = \sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{im}^{(t)},
  \label{eq:mstep_pi}
\end{equation}
and a weighted reconstruction problem for each basis:
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\Phi_m}
  \sum_{i=1}^{N} \gamma_{im}^{(t)}\,\|s^{(i)} - \hat{s}_m^{(i)}\|^2,
  \label{eq:mstep_phi}
\end{equation}
solved via \textsc{WeightedNeuralPOD} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:npod}).
Because this subproblem is minimized by gradient descent rather than
to global optimality, TEMPO is a Generalized EM
algorithm~\cite{Neal1998}: it guarantees monotone non-decrease of the
observed-data log-likelihood at every iteration under the
sufficient condition that each M-step does not decrease the
Q-function.

\paragraph{Adaptive basis update.}
Retraining all $M$ NeuralPOD bases at every EM iteration is
computationally prohibitive.
We introduce a distribution-shift criterion to determine whether each
basis requires updating.
Let $\alpha^{(i)}$ denote the global POD projection of $s^{(i)}$,
computed once during initialization and fixed thereafter.
The responsibility-weighted first and second moments of regime $m$ are
\begin{align}
  \mu_m^{(t)}
  &= \frac{1}{N_m^{(t)}}\sum_{i=1}^N
    \gamma_{im}^{(t)}\,\alpha^{(i)},
  \label{eq:mu}\\
  \Sigma_m^{(t)}
  &= \frac{1}{N_m^{(t)}}\sum_{i=1}^N
    \gamma_{im}^{(t)}\,
    (\alpha^{(i)}-\mu_m^{(t)})(\alpha^{(i)}-\mu_m^{(t)})^\top.
  \label{eq:Sigma}
\end{align}
The distribution-shift criterion measures the relative change in these
moments between consecutive iterations:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_m^{(t)}
  = \frac{\|\mu_m^{(t)} - \mu_m^{(t-1)}\|_2}
         {\|\mu_m^{(t-1)}\|_2 + \epsilon}
  + \frac{\|\Sigma_m^{(t)} - \Sigma_m^{(t-1)}\|_F}
         {\|\Sigma_m^{(t-1)}\|_F + \epsilon}.
  \label{eq:Delta}
\end{equation}
Note that $\Delta_m^{(t)}$ tracks the shift in the \emph{data content}
of regime $m$ — the snapshots it effectively owns — not merely
the volume of responsibility reassignments.
Two snapshots with very different $\alpha^{(i)}$ swapping regime
membership produce a large $\Delta_m^{(t)}$, triggering a basis
update; two similar snapshots doing the same do not.
Given thresholds $0 < \varepsilon_s < \varepsilon_l$, the update
decision at iteration $t$ follows a three-way rule:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Condition & Action & Rationale \\
\midrule
$\Delta_m^{(t)} < \varepsilon_s$
  & \textsc{Skip}
  & Data manifold unchanged; basis remains accurate \\
$\varepsilon_s \le \Delta_m^{(t)} < \varepsilon_l$
  & \textsc{Incremental}
  & Moderate drift; add or prune one mode \\
$\Delta_m^{(t)} \ge \varepsilon_l$
  & \textsc{Full rerun}
  & Large shift; rebuild from random initialization \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
In the \textsc{Incremental} case, we compute the residual of the
current basis under the updated responsibilities $\gamma^{(t)}$, add
a new mode if $\|r_m^{(P_m)}\|_{\gamma_m^{(t)}}^2 \ge \mathrm{Tol}_m$,
and prune the last mode if its weighted contribution falls below
$\varepsilon_p$.
The \textsc{Full rerun} case uses a cold-start random initialization.
When $\Delta_m^{(t)} \ge \varepsilon_l$, the parameters
$\Phi_m^{(t-1)}$ are a local minimum of the \emph{previous}
weighted objective and may be in a basin that is entirely
misaligned with the new weighted objective; warm-starting would
trap gradient descent there, encoding the old regime's features
in the new basis.

\paragraph{Convergence and model selection.}
EM terminates when $\max_m \Delta_m^{(t)} < \varepsilon_c$.
The number of regimes $M \in \{2,3,4\}$ is selected by jointly
minimizing BIC on the GMM fit and mean assignment entropy;
see Table~\ref{tab:regime_selection}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\textsc{WeightedNeuralPOD}$\bigl(
  \{s^{(i)}, t^{(i)}, \kappa^{(i)}, \gamma_{im}\}_{i=1}^N,\;
  \mathrm{Tol}_m\bigr)$}
\label{alg:npod}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Train $\phi_0^{(m)}(\cdot\,;\kappa)$ by minimizing
  $\sum_i \gamma_{im}\,
  \|s^{(i)} - \phi_0^{(m)}(\cdot\,;\kappa^{(i)})\|^2$
\State $r^{(0),i} \leftarrow
  s^{(i)} - \phi_0^{(m)}(\cdot\,;\kappa^{(i)})$
  for all $i$;\enspace $p \leftarrow 0$
\While{$\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^N
  \gamma_{im}\,\|r^{(p),i}\|^2 \;\ge\; \mathrm{Tol}_m$}
  \State $p \leftarrow p + 1$
  \State Randomly initialize
    $\phi_p^{(m)}(\cdot\,;\kappa)$ and $\psi_p^{(m)}(\cdot\,;\kappa)$
  \State Minimize $\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^N \gamma_{im}
    \bigl\|r^{(p-1),i}
    - \phi_p^{(m)}(\cdot\,;\kappa^{(i)})\,
      \psi_p^{(m)}(t^{(i)};\kappa^{(i)})\bigr\|^2$
    over $\phi_p^{(m)}, \psi_p^{(m)}$
  \State $r^{(p),i} \leftarrow r^{(p-1),i}
    - \phi_p^{(m)}(\cdot\,;\kappa^{(i)})\,
      \psi_p^{(m)}(t^{(i)};\kappa^{(i)})$
    \enspace for all $i$
\EndWhile
\State \Return $P_m \!\leftarrow\! p$,\enspace
  $\phi_0^{(m)}$,\enspace
  $\bigl\{\phi_p^{(m)},\psi_p^{(m)}\bigr\}_{p=1}^{P_m}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{TEMPO --- Offline Phase}
\label{alg:tempo}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\mathcal{D} = \{s^{(i)}, t^{(i)}, \kappa^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^N$;\;
  $M,\;\sigma^2,\;\varepsilon_s,\;\varepsilon_l,\;\varepsilon_p,\;
  \varepsilon_c,\;\{\mathrm{Tol}_m\}_{m=1}^M$
\Ensure Regime bases $\{\Phi_m^*\}$,\;
  responsibilities $\{\gamma_{im}^*\}$,\;
  weights $\{\pi_m^*\}$
%
\Statex \textit{Initialization}
\State Compute global POD on $\{s^{(i)}\}$;
  project to $\alpha^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^P$ for all $i$
\State Run GMM-EM on $\{\alpha^{(i)}\}$ $\to$
  $\gamma_{im}^{(0)},\;\pi_m^{(0)},\;\mu_m^{(0)},\;\Sigma_m^{(0)}$
\For{$m = 1,\ldots,M$}
  \State $\Phi_m^{(0)} \leftarrow$
    \Call{WeightedNeuralPOD}
    {$\{s^{(i)},t^{(i)},\kappa^{(i)},\gamma_{im}^{(0)}\},\,\mathrm{Tol}_m$}
\EndFor
%
\Statex \textit{EM iterations}
\Repeat
  \Statex \quad\textit{E-step}
  \For{all $i = 1,\ldots,N$ and $m = 1,\ldots,M$}
    \State Compute $\hat{s}_m^{(i)}$ via~\eqref{eq:neuralpod_regime}
      using $\Phi_m^{(t-1)}$ and $\kappa^{(i)}$
    \State $\gamma_{im}^{(t)} \leftarrow$ Eq.~\eqref{eq:estep}
  \EndFor
  \Statex \quad\textit{M-step: mixture weights}
  \State $\pi_m^{(t)} \leftarrow N_m^{(t)}/N$
    for all $m$ \hfill\eqref{eq:mstep_pi}
  \Statex \quad\textit{M-step: bases}
  \For{$m = 1,\ldots,M$}
    \State Compute $\mu_m^{(t)}$~\eqref{eq:mu},\;
      $\Sigma_m^{(t)}$~\eqref{eq:Sigma},\;
      $\Delta_m^{(t)}$~\eqref{eq:Delta}
    \If{$\Delta_m^{(t)} < \varepsilon_s$}
      \State $\Phi_m^{(t)} \leftarrow \Phi_m^{(t-1)}$
        \hfill \textsc{Skip}
    \ElsIf{$\Delta_m^{(t)} < \varepsilon_l$}
      \hfill \textsc{Incremental}
      \State Compute $r_m^{(P_m)}$ under updated $\gamma^{(t)}$
      \If{$\|r_m^{(P_m)}\|_{\gamma_m^{(t)}}^2 \ge \mathrm{Tol}_m$}
        \State Train mode $P_m{+}1$;\; $P_m \leftarrow P_m + 1$
      \EndIf
      \If{weighted contribution of mode $P_m < \varepsilon_p$}
        \State $P_m \leftarrow P_m - 1$
      \EndIf
    \Else
      \hfill \textsc{Full rerun}
      \State $\Phi_m^{(t)} \leftarrow$
        \Call{WeightedNeuralPOD}
        {$\{s^{(i)},t^{(i)},\kappa^{(i)},\gamma_{im}^{(t)}\},\,\mathrm{Tol}_m$}
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
\Until{$\max_m \Delta_m^{(t)} < \varepsilon_c$}
\State \Return $\Phi_m^* \!\leftarrow\! \Phi_m^{(t)}$,\;
  $\gamma_{im}^* \!\leftarrow\! \gamma_{im}^{(t)}$,\;
  $\pi_m^* \!\leftarrow\! \pi_m^{(t)}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\subsection{TEMPO: Online Gated Operator}\label{sec:online}


After Algorithm~\ref{alg:tempo} converges, all NeuralPOD bases
$\{\Phi_m^*\}$ and responsibilities $\{\gamma_{im}^*\}$ are frozen.
The online phase trains a \textbf{GatingNet}
$\mathcal{W}: \mathbb{R}^{m_s + d_\kappa} \to \Delta^{M-1}$
and $M$ \textbf{BranchNets}
$\mathcal{B}_m: \mathbb{R}^{m_s + d_\kappa} \to \mathbb{R}^{P_m}$,
each mapping sensor observations $u_0}$ and parameter
$\kappa$ to regime weights and expansion coefficients, respectively.
The TEMPO prediction at query point $x \in \Omega$ is
\begin{equation}
  \hat{s}(x;\,u_0,\kappa)
  = \sum_{m=1}^{M} w_m(u_0,\kappa)
    \left[
      \phi_0^{(m)}\!(x;\kappa)
      + \sum_{k=1}^{P_m}
        b_{m,k}(u_0,\kappa)\;
        \phi_k^{(m)}\!(x;\kappa)
    \right],
  \label{eq:predict}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{w} = \mathcal{W}(u_0,\kappa)$ and
$\mathbf{b}_m = \mathcal{B}_m(u_0,\kappa)$.
The prediction is mesh-free and evaluable at any $x \in \Omega$
without retraining.

\paragraph{Training objective.}
Writing $w_m^{(i)} = w_m(u_0^{(i)},\kappa^{(i)})$, the online loss is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{online}}
  = \underbrace{
      \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
      \|s^{(i)} - \hat{s}(\cdot;\,u_0^{(i)},\kappa^{(i)})\|^2
    }_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}}
  +\;\lambda_{\mathrm{KL}}
    \underbrace{
      \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,m}
      w_m^{(i)}\log\frac{w_m^{(i)}}{\gamma_{im}^*}
    }_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}}}
  -\;\lambda_{H}
    \underbrace{
      \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,m}
      w_m^{(i)}\log w_m^{(i)}
    }_{-\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ent}}}.
  \label{eq:online_loss}
\end{equation}
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}$ drives reconstruction accuracy.
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}}$ anchors the online gating to the
offline regime partition: it penalizes deviation of $\mathbf{w}^{(i)}$
from the EM responsibilities $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_i^*$, so the
network cannot discard the discovered structure in pursuit of a
simpler routing.
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ent}}$ prevents collapse to a single expert,
ensuring all $M$ regime bases contribute.
The hyperparameters $\lambda_{\mathrm{KL}}, \lambda_H \ge 0$ trade
off these objectives.

\section{Computational Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

We evaluate TEMPO on parametric PDE benchmarks, verifying
that (i) the offline phase discovers interpretable regime
structure, (ii) the learned bases faithfully represent
regime-specific solution features, and (iii) TEMPO achieves
lower reconstruction error than single-basis baselines.

\paragraph{Datasets.}
We use the PDEBench benchmark~\cite{Takamoto2022}
across three benchmark problems.

\textbf{1D Burgers' equation.}
\begin{equation}
  u_t + u\,u_x = \nu\,u_{xx},
  \qquad x\in(-1,1),\quad t\in(0,2],
  \label{eq:burgers}
\end{equation}
with periodic boundary conditions and random initial conditions
sampled from a fixed distribution.
The viscosity $\nu \in \{0.001,\,0.01,\,0.1,\,1.0\}$ spans
qualitatively distinct regimes: smooth diffusive profiles at
$\nu=1.0$ and near-discontinuous shock structures at $\nu=0.001$.
Each value provides $N=9{,}500$ trajectories on a uniform grid
of $N_x=1024$ spatial points and $N_t=101$ time steps,
yielding $38{,}000$ trajectories in total.

\textbf{2D Darcy Flow.}
\begin{equation}
  -\nabla\cdot\bigl(a(x)\,\nabla u(x)\bigr) = f(x),
  \qquad x\in(0,1)^2,
  \label{eq:darcy}
\end{equation}
with zero Dirichlet boundary conditions

% TODO: 1D Advection

\paragraph{Methods.}
We compare five methods.
As baselines we use DeepONet~\cite{Lu2019},
POD-DeepONet~\cite{Lu2022}, and Neural-POD~\cite{Mou2026}.
TEMPO is evaluated in two variants depending on the basis
used in the offline phase:
\textbf{TEMPO(POD)} constructs regime-specific bases via
weighted SVD, and
\textbf{TEMPO(NeuralPOD)} replaces them with Fourier
NeuralPOD bases~\cite{Mou2026}, which capture nonlinear
and parameter-dependent solution structure.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
We report the mean relative $L^2$ error
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon = \frac{1}{N_\mathrm{test}}
  \sum_{i=1}^{N_\mathrm{test}}
  \frac{\norm{s^{(i)} - \hat{s}(\,\cdot\,;\,u^{(i)},\kappa^{(i)})}}
       {\norm{s^{(i)}}},
  \label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
together with RMSE and MSE, computed per viscosity value
and aggregated over all $\nu$.

\paragraph{Selection of $M$.}
The number of regimes is chosen from $M\in\{2,3,4\}$ by
jointly minimising BIC on the EM-GMM fit and mean assignment
entropy $H = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,m}\gamma_{im}\log\gamma_{im}$;
lower entropy indicates more confident regime assignments.
Table~\ref{tab:regime_selection} reports both criteria;
$M=3$ achieves the best trade-off and is used throughout.
